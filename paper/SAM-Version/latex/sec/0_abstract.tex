\begin{abstract}
SAM's ViT-H encoder was pre-trained on over one billion masks, yet on COD10K's camouflaged test set it achieves only 57.2\% mIoU with center-of-mass prompts and 20.5\% with edge prompts. We ask whether the decoder is the bottleneck. Freezing the encoder and fine-tuning only the 4M-parameter mask decoder on 3,040 training images raises mIoU to 73.8\% (+16.6 points), and the gain transfers across all four prompt types; edge prompts improve from 20.5\% to 69.4\%. Mixed-prompt training (point/box) is responsible for this robustness: it outperforms point-only by up to 4 mIoU and box-only by 19--37 mIoU. A 31K-parameter Learnable Local Preprocessing Module (LLPM), placed before the frozen encoder, adds +2.6 mIoU on edge prompts by sharpening boundary contrast in input space. The decoder generalizes without retraining: on CAMO and NC4K it improves mIoU by +18.8 and +18.5 points over base SAM. Error analysis shows the remaining failures concentrate in dark images, tiny objects, and boundary-ambiguous cases. The decoder-only variant requires no architectural changes, trains in 90 minutes on one GPU, and produces a 16MB checkpoint.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Foundation segmentation models like SAM encode rich visual features but struggle on camouflaged objects, where foreground and background differ only by subtle texture and boundary cues.
We find that decoder adaptation is the primary lever for improving SAM in this setting: freezing SAM's ViT-H encoder and fine-tuning only the 4M-parameter mask decoder on COD10K raises mIoU from 57.2\% to 73.8\% (+16.6 points).
This gain is prompt-robust, transferring across four prompt types including edge prompts (+238\% relative).
We validate prompt robustness through ablation: mixed-prompt training (point/box) outperforms point-only by up to 4 mIoU and box-only by 19--37 mIoU.
A complementary Learnable Local Preprocessing Module (LLPM, $\sim$31K parameters) enhances input images before the frozen encoder, yielding a further +2.6 mIoU on edge prompts.
The decoder generalizes without retraining: evaluated on CAMO and NC4K, it improves mIoU by +18.8 and +18.5 points respectively over base SAM, confirming that specialization learns transferable camouflage cues rather than dataset-specific patterns.
Error analysis reveals that remaining failures cluster in dark images, tiny objects, and boundary-ambiguous cases---the LLPM specifically addresses the latter.
The decoder-only variant requires no architectural changes, trains in under 2 hours on a single GPU, and produces a 16MB checkpoint; the LLPM adds a lightweight pre-encoder module for further gains on boundary-sensitive cases.
\end{abstract}
